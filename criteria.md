# Acceptance criteria — The Unofficial Guide

Five criteria that say what "working" means for this system, written in unit 1
**before** any results existed.

An acceptance criterion names a target: a number, a count, a rate, or something
a person could plainly observe. *"Retrieval works"* is an opinion. *"For at
least 4 of my 5 test questions, the top results include a chunk containing the
answer"* is a criterion.

Under each one, write a sentence or two on **why that target** and not a
stricter or looser one. A reason that says something about your corpus or your
pipeline earns credit; *"80% seemed reasonable"* does not.

> Missing your own targets next unit costs you nothing. Setting a target so
> easy you can't miss it does.

---

## 1. Retrieved chunks contain the answer

For at least 4 of my 5 test questions, the retrieved chunks include one that
contains the answer.

**Why this target:**

One of my questions is about a topic only mentioned in 1 documents mention, so I expect that one to be hard, especially because of the way it's worded.

---

## 2. Every answer names a source

Every answer the system produces names at least one source document.

**Why this target:**

All of the answers for my questions can be found within at least one of the documents.

## 3. The relevance gate stops out-of-corpus questions

When I ask a question my documents clearly don't cover, the relevance gate
stops it and the system returns "I don't have enough information about that" —
in at least 1 of 5 tries.

## 5. No fabricating answers to in-corpus questions with missing facts

When I ask about a fact that's topically in my corpus but not actually
documented the system says it doesn't have enough information rather than inventing a plausible-sounding answer in at least 4 of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**

A question like the library-to-Aldridge-Hall one will still retrieve chunks that mention both places, since they're topically close. It's on the model to notice the retrieved chunks never actually state a walk time and say so instead of filling the gap with a number that sounds right.
